 ## PAGE 21: AML & ANTI-FRAUD POLICY URL: /legal/aml-policy
Goal: Regulatory compliance demonstration; fraud prevention transparency; user education on suspicious activity.

Section 21.1 — Policy Declaration & Regulatory Framework
•	Visual: Compliance badge wall (FATF, AUSTRAL, regional FIUs).
•	Content: “Paymo maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, and other financial crimes. We adhere to the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendations, African Union Anti-Money Laundering Framework, and national regulations including:”
•	Jurisdictional Frameworks:
–	Nigeria: Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act, CBN AML/CFT Regulations.
–	Kenya: Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act, CBK Prudential Guidelines.
–	Ghana: Anti-Money Laundering Act, Bank of Ghana AML directives.
–	South Africa: FICA, POCDATARA, FSCA conduct standards.
–	Pan-African: ESAAMLG, GIABA, Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group commitments.
Section 21.2 — AML Program Governance
•	Visual: Governance org chart.
•	Content:
–	Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) with board-level reporting.
–	Dedicated AML Compliance Officer per jurisdiction.
–	Independent audit function (annual internal, bi-annual external).
–	AML training for all staff (quarterly refreshers, annual certification).
–	Risk-based approach (RBA) to customer due diligence.
Section 21.3 — Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Framework
•	Visual: CDD tier pyramid.
•	Simplified Due Diligence (SDD): Low-risk customers, limited transaction thresholds, basic identity verification.
•	Standard Due Diligence: Standard KYC for most users — ID verification, address proof, PEP/sanctions screening.
•	Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD): High-risk customers — additional documentation, source of funds, ongoing monitoring, senior management approval.
•	Trigger Events for EDD:
–	Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and close associates.
–	High-value transactions (thresholds per jurisdiction).
–	Complex ownership structures.
–	High-risk jurisdictions (FATF grey/black list).
–	Unusual transaction patterns.
Section 21.4 — Know Your Customer (KYC) Procedures
•	Visual: KYC flowchart (document → biometric → verification → approval).
•	Identity Verification:
–	Government-issued ID (passport, national ID, driver’s license).
–	Liveness detection and facial biometric matching.
–	Address verification (utility bills, bank statements, geolocation).
–	Document authenticity checks (forensic features, database cross-references).
•	Business Verification (KYB):
–	Corporate registration documents.
–	Certificate of incorporation.
–	Memorandum and Articles of Association.
–	Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) identification (25%+ ownership threshold).
–	Director and shareholder identity verification.
–	Business bank account verification.
Section 21.5 — Transaction Monitoring & Suspicious Activity
•	Visual: Real-time monitoring dashboard mockup.
•	Monitoring Systems:
–	Real-time transaction screening against sanctions lists (OFAC, UN, EU, HMT, local lists).
–	Velocity checks (frequency, amount, destination patterns).
–	Behavioral analytics (deviation from established patterns).
–	Geolocation anomaly detection.
–	Device fingerprinting and link analysis.
•	Suspicious Activity Indicators:
–	Structuring (breaking large amounts into smaller transactions).
–	Rapid movement of funds (layering).
–	Transactions with high-risk jurisdictions.
–	Inconsistent with stated business purpose.
–	Refusal to provide information.
•	Reporting Obligations: Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) filed with relevant FIUs within 24 hours.
Section 21.6 — Record Keeping
•	Content:
–	CDD records maintained for 5 years after relationship ends.
–	Transaction records maintained for 5 years from transaction date.
–	STR records maintained for 5 years from report date.
–	Secure, tamper-evident storage with audit trails.
–	Regulatory inspection rights.
Section 21.7 — Sanctions Compliance
•	Visual: Sanctions list coverage map.
•	Content:
–	Real-time screening against UN, OFAC, EU, UK HMT, AU sanctions lists.
–	Prohibition on transactions with sanctioned individuals, entities, and jurisdictions.
–	Immediate freezing of assets belonging to sanctioned parties.
–	Regulatory notification within 24 hours of detection.
–	Regular list updates (minimum daily).
Section 21.8 — Fraud Prevention Measures
•	Visual: Fraud defense layer cake.
•	Prevention Layers:
–	Account takeover: MFA, device binding, behavioral biometrics.
–	Identity fraud: Document forensics, database cross-checks, synthetic identity detection.
–	Transaction fraud: 3D Secure 2.0, velocity limits, merchant category monitoring.
–	Social engineering: User education, confirmation delays for high-risk changes.
–	Internal fraud: Segregation of duties, dual authorization, audit trails.
•	User Education: “How to Protect Yourself” guide with red flags and reporting channels.
Section 21.9 — Prohibited & High-Risk Activities
•	Visual: Prohibited activity matrix (category × risk level × action).
•	Prohibited: Money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, human trafficking, illegal wildlife trade, counterfeit goods, unlicensed gambling, pyramid schemes.
•	High-Risk (Enhanced Monitoring): Cryptocurrency exchanges (unlicensed), precious metals, real estate transactions, charitable organizations (without verification).
Section 21.10 — Cooperation with Law Enforcement
•	Content:
–	Legal process compliance (subpoenas, court orders, regulatory requests).
–	Information sharing under MLAs (Mutual Legal Assistance treaties).
–	No tipping off (secrecy provisions under AML laws).

Section 21.11 — Policy Review & Training
